## Entry into the EU Insurance Market: Feasibility & Strategic Challenges
## Module 1 – Mission, Strategy & Organizational Structure
_(Focus: ecosystem logic vs. EU regulatory scrutiny)_
### 容子咏 – Mission & Strategy: “1+N” Ecosystem Model (1.5min)

**Key focus:** Ping An is not a product-based insurer, but a **customer-centered ecosystem**

- **Mission & Vision**
    
    - From “insurance provider” to **integrated financial + healthcare service platform**
        
    - Long-term vision: lifecycle management of customers' financial and health needs
        
- **Core Strategy: “1+N”**
    
    - One customer → multiple financial and healthcare products
        
    - Strong cross-selling and data integration across insurance, banking, asset management
        
- **EU relevance & tension**
    
    - In the EU, such “ecosystem bundling” faces:
        
        - Fair competition scrutiny
            
        - Data separation and anti-monopoly concerns
            
    - Sets up a key analytical question:
        
        - Can Ping An's ecosystem logic survive EU-style regulatory fragmentation?

### 花可谦 – Organizational Structure: Platform vs. Traditional Insurer (1.5min)

**Key focus:** Technology as a horizontal capability, not a support function

- **Group structure overview**
    
    - Financial subsidiaries (insurance, banking, asset management)
        
    - Technology subsidiaries as horizontal enablers
        
- **Role of Ping An Tech**
    
    - AI, big data, risk modeling, cloud infrastructure
        
    - Supports all financial business lines simultaneously
        
- **Comparison with EU incumbents**
    
    - Traditional EU insurers (e.g. Allianz) are more vertically siloed
        
    - Ping An's matrix-style structure is unusual in Europe
        
- **Regulatory implication**
    
    - EU regulators prefer clear functional separation
        
    - Ping An's structure may increase compliance and supervisory complexity

## Module 2 – Products & Services

_(Focus: differentiation + local feasibility)_

### 吴宇韬 – “Healthcare + Pension” as Core Differentiation (3-4min)

**Key focus:** Matching Ping An's strengths with EU demographic pressures

- **Core product domains**
    
    - Health insurance and health management
        
    - Pension and long-term care insurance
        
- **Healthcare logic**
    
    - Integration of insurance with online medical consultation
        
    - Potential to reduce claim costs via early diagnosis and preventive care
        
- **EU context**
    
    - Strong public healthcare, but:
        
        - Aging population
            
        - Efficiency bottlenecks in public systems
            
    - Digital health solutions are increasingly attractive
        
- **Critical localization constraints**
    
    - Extremely high privacy sensitivity
        
    - Practices common in China (e.g. facial recognition, aggressive data integration) may be unacceptable
        
    - Products must be redesigned under GDPR-by-default logic

## Module 3 – Financial Performance & Capital Adequacy

_(Focus: professionalism & international comparability)_

### Speaker 4 – Financials, IFRS 17 & Solvency Alignment

**Key focus:** Entry feasibility is not about scale, but about regulatory compatibility

- **Accounting standards**
    
    - Introduction of **IFRS 17**
        
    - Enhances international comparability of insurance liabilities
        
    - Both China and EU are transitioning to this framework
        
- **Profitability indicators**
    
    - Emphasis on **Operating Profit**
        
    - More accurate reflection of insurance core business performance than net profit
        
- **Capital adequacy**
    
    - Comparison:
        
        - China: C-ROSS (China Risk-Oriented Solvency System)
            
        - EU: **Solvency II**
            
    - Conclusion:
        
        - Ping An's capital position meets international standards
            
        - Main challenge lies in **regulatory alignment**, not capital shortage
            
- **Implication for EU entry**
    
    - Financial capacity is sufficient
        
    - Compliance costs and reporting complexity will be high

## Module 4 – EU Market, Regulation & SWOT

_(Focus: regulatory friction and strategic realism)_

### 吕昊阳 – EU Regulatory Environment: Where Conflicts Arise

**Key focus:** Data and sustainability as gatekeepers

- **Data protection**
    
    - **GDPR** as a structural constraint
        
    - Ping An's data-driven risk pricing faces:
        
        - Consent limitations
            
        - Purpose limitation
            
        - Restrictions on automated profiling
            
- **ESG requirements**
    
    - EU insurance sector strongly aligned with sustainable finance
        
    - Ping An's recent ESG initiatives:
        
        - Green finance
            
        - Climate-related insurance
            
    - ESG can act as a legitimacy signal for EU entry

### Speaker 6 – SWOT Analysis & Entry Strategy

**SWOT summary (high-level)**

- **Strengths**
    
    - Advanced AI and risk modeling
        
    - Integrated financial–health ecosystem
        
- **Weaknesses**
    
    - Low EU brand recognition
        
    - High compliance and localization costs
        
- **Opportunities**
    
    - Aging population
        
    - Growth of private pensions and digital health insurance
        
- **Threats**
    
    - Strong incumbents (e.g. AXA)
        
    - Political sensitivity and foreign investment screening
        

**Strategic entry recommendation**

- Avoid capital-intensive physical expansion
    
- Prefer:
    
    - Digital joint ventures
        
    - Technology licensing and AI empowerment
        
    - Acquisition of a mid-sized EU digital insurer as a pilot
        

**Final conclusion**

- Ping An's challenge in the EU is not technological or financial
    
- It is fundamentally **institutional and regulatory compatibility**
